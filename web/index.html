Your personal blood sugar toolkit

GlucoseAnywhere is a free set of blood sugar tools that runs in your browser on any phone, tablet or computer. It starts with your live Dexcom reading, a trend arrow and a chart, and adds optional tools for logging insulin and carbs. It was built to manage one person's diabetes day to day and is shared so others can use it too.

  • Live glucose. A big, always-on display for a phone, a spare tablet or a PC. Chart view puts the graph first, and big-number mode fills the screen and keeps it awake.
  • Insulin and carb log (optional). Log doses and meals, including ones from earlier, and edit or delete entries any time. Your full history stays on your device.
  • Insulin on board (optional). See how much insulin is still working and how fast, from your own insulin's peak time and duration.
  • Bolus calculator (optional). A pump-style estimate from your own carb ratio, correction factor and target. It stays off until you enter your own numbers.
  • You keep your data. Everything is stored on your device. Optionally keep a copy of the insulin log and your dose settings in your own Google Drive or OneDrive, or export the log as a file. Google Drive sync is in development and testing, so for now only approved testers can connect.
  • Light and dark themes, a layout built for phones, and one that uses the whole window on a desktop. Add it to your home screen like an app.
  • Free, no ads, no account. Open source under the MIT license.

What you need

The Dexcom account of the person wearing the sensor, with Share turned on and at least one follower in the Dexcom app. A follower's login won't work.

Your privacy

Your sign-in and readings are stored on your own device and sent only to Dexcom, to sign in and fetch readings. If you turn on the optional insulin log, it is stored on your device too, and you can choose to link your own Google Drive or OneDrive account to keep a copy there. You sign in to that account yourself, only the insulin log, your dose settings and your medication buttons are sent (never your Dexcom sign-in or readings), it goes straight from your device to your drive and not through us, and you can disconnect at any time. GlucoseAnywhere has no accounts and adds no ads, analytics or tracking to the app. Like any website, our host (Cloudflare) can see that a page was requested, including your IP address and browser, and gives us simple traffic counts. You don't have to take our word for it: here's how to check, and the source code is public.

Important

Not a medical device. Don't use it in place of the Dexcom app's alarms. It has no alerts. The optional insulin calculator is only a rough estimate from numbers you enter yourself, so check every dose against your care team's guidance. GlucoseAnywhere is not affiliated with or endorsed by Dexcom, and it uses Dexcom's unofficial Share service, which can change or stop working at any time.

    Wall display

    Use a spare tablet or screen as an always-on readout. Use the expand button on the home screen for big-number mode. It fills the window; tap the screen to show its controls, including Fullscreen.

    Keyboard: C chart view, B big number, F fullscreen, R refresh, Esc close. Open glucoseanywhere.com/#chart to start in chart view.

    Setup tips for a wall or desk tablet
    • Install it. Android: Chrome menu, Add to Home screen (or Install app). iPad: Share, Add to Home Screen. It then opens without browser bars.
    • Keep it awake. With Stay awake on, big-number and chart views ask the browser to keep the screen on. If the status above says it can't, set the device's screen timeout to Never (Android: Settings, Display; iPad: Settings, Display & Brightness, Auto-Lock) and leave it plugged in.
    • Lock it to the app. iPad: Guided Access (Settings, Accessibility). Android: Screen pinning. This stops accidental exits.
    • Auto-start. Turn on the option above, and the app will open straight to the big number each time. Android also has a "Big display" shortcut when you long-press the app icon.
    • Dim it at night with the device's brightness setting. The big display is black, so it's easy on the eyes and on OLED screens.
    • If the number is crossed out, the last reading is over 15 minutes old: check Wi-Fi, or that the sensor is still sending to the phone.

    About

    Not a medical device. GlucoseAnywhere is not affiliated with or endorsed by Dexcom. The glucose display is not for treatment decisions or in place of the Dexcom app's alarms. The optional insulin calculator is only a rough estimate from the numbers you enter: confirm every dose with your care team's guidance. It uses Dexcom's unofficial Share service, which can change or stop working at any time.

    Privacy. Your data stays under your control. Your sign-in and readings are stored on this device and sent only to Dexcom, to sign in and fetch readings. The optional insulin log is stored on this device too. If you choose to link your own Google Drive or OneDrive, you sign in to that account yourself and only the insulin log, your dose settings and your medication buttons are sent there, straight from this device to your drive (never through us), until you disconnect. GlucoseAnywhere has no accounts and adds no analytics or tracking to the app. Like any website, our host (Cloudflare) can see that this site's pages and files were requested, including your IP address and browser, and gives us simple traffic counts.

    Your password is saved on this device so the app can sign in again when Dexcom's session expires. Anyone who can use this browser profile can read it. On a shared computer, turn off "Remember me" or use "Forget everything" when you're done.

    Not synced. Each browser or device keeps its own sign-in, history and insulin log (you can export and import the log). Clearing site data deletes it.

    Verify it yourself

    1. Open your browser's developer tools (F12, or Cmd+Option+I on a Mac) and choose the Network tab.
    2. Sign in. You should see requests only to share2.dexcom.com, shareous1.dexcom.com or share.dexcom.jp, plus this site's own files. Nothing else, unless you have linked your own Google Drive or OneDrive, which adds googleapis.com, graph.microsoft.com or login.microsoftonline.com.
    3. Look at this page's Content-Security-Policy response header. It tells your browser to block every connection except those Dexcom servers.
    4. Turn on airplane mode and reopen the app. Your saved readings still appear, because they're stored on this device.
    5. Read the code. Only dexcom.js contacts Dexcom, only the optional sync.js contacts Google or Microsoft, and only store.js saves your data. The small sw.js file keeps an offline copy of this app's own files and nothing else.
